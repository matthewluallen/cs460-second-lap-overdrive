SECOND LAP: CS460 OVERDRIVE CAPSTONE
Four teams, 14-16 students, four classes of 170 minutes each.

Casey and Matt are considering Rowan Veyl's fictional investment proposal.
Assess what works, which claims withstand scrutiny, what can go wrong, what
should change, and under what measurable conditions you would fund the project.
Each student receives a course kit. Troubleshooting older hardware is part of
the investigation; document observations and compare with a working reference.
The included controller runs a local simulator. Physical integration is separate
work and must be validated on the actual assigned car, firmware and host.

EVERY TEAM ADDRESSES SIX AREAS
1. System and operation: architecture, trust boundaries, protocols, sensors,
   actuators, control logic, dependencies and measured behavior.
2. Repository and provenance: code, history, component origins, licenses,
   releases, tests and evidence supporting claimed capabilities.
3. Security: threat model, reproducible weaknesses, impact, incident analysis,
   corrections, recovery and verification including legitimate operation.
4. Founder and public evidence: source-supported timeline, corroboration,
   contradictions and competing explanations. Distinguish claims from facts.
5. Business and operations: rights, service ownership, supplier dependencies,
   support, manufacturing assertions and exit or recovery conditions.
6. AI and assurance: assess the founder chatbot's answers and sources. Explain
   uncertainty, unsupported responses and the limits of automated assurance.

Develop greater depth in a primary contribution area and a connected area.
Possible contributions include protocol tools, a validated adapter, control
logic, update verification, telemetry, tests, evidence tooling or AI assurance.
Interfaces are agreed with the other teams so contributions can be integrated.
There is no required single solution and no requirement to find every issue.

FOUR CLASSES
1. Pitch, kit triage, baseline, protocol observation, initial threat model,
   task allocation, evidence logs and founder questions.
2. Code/protocol investigation, public research, interviews, reproductions,
   corrective work and review of commercial claims.
3. Scoped incident/emulation, mitigation and retest, peer integration,
   contradictory evidence review and investment recommendation.
4. Comprehensive team defenses, integrated demonstration and debrief.
Pre-reads are assigned before each session. Exact dates are announced in class.

SUBMIT
- Integrated team assessment covering the six areas, findings ranked by impact
  and confidence, residual uncertainty and a defended investment recommendation.
- Evidence register linking claims to source, date/version, method, observation,
  alternative explanations and the person who performed the work.
- Reviewable engineering contribution with an interface, reproducible tests or
  measurements and evidence of peer review and integration.
- Individual record of substantial investigation, reasoning, validation and
  cross-checking of another student's work. One isolated finding is insufficient.
- Presentation: 30 minutes per team. Allow 20 minutes for analysis (five minutes
  per student in a four-person team), three for demonstration, five for questions
  and two for transition. Three-person teams use the extra five minutes for
  synthesis or questions. Every student owns a substantive section.

Use CWE for supported implementation weaknesses, ATT&CK for adversary behavior,
CAPEC for attack patterns and ATLAS for adversarial AI behavior. CALDERA is an
emulation platform. Explain mappings and record source versions; labels alone
are not findings. Business uncertainty need not have a CWE identifier.

AI use is optional and does not affect access to full credit. If you use AI,
record material prompts, relevant output, mistakes and independent validation.
All students can examine the supplied persona through transcripts if desired.
Hints support progress without reducing mastery credit. Supported negative
results and well-explained hardware faults are useful evidence.

Keep testing within assigned course software and authorized kits. Read
SIMULATION_NOTICE.txt. Public research is read-only; do not contact real people
as the fictional founder or treat fictional business records as real evidence.
